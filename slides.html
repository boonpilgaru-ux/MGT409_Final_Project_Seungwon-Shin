02 · What is new here

The difference is the connected sequence.

The app combines familiar patterns. It makes no claim that preference questions, shopping search, or generative design are new by themselves.

Tee:dentity prototype

Starts with taste choices, then drafts a design brief and quote.

Graphic tee marketplaces
Offer broad catalogues from brands and artists.

Searches after the profile and explains why results may fit.

Shopping research assistants
Ask about needs and summarize candidate products.

06 · Safety

Defenses target what the app reads and creates.

19 / 19local safety regression cases passed

Prompt injection in context

External text is delimited as untrusted data; the test checks the prompt boundary.

Unsafe URL fetching

Private/local IP targets and redirects to private addresses are blocked.

Malicious generated SVG

Scripts, event handlers, foreign objects, and external references are removed or rejected.

Evidence boundary: tests use local fakes. They verify application checks, not live-provider refusal. The per-IP rate limit is not an aggregate spending cap; generated profiles and retailer details can still be wrong or stale.

08 · Limitations + next steps

Next, measure whether the guided flow helps.

The project is ready for a small usability test. It is a local student prototype, not a deployed commerce service.

Evidence still needed

  • Consented real-user feedback and usability notes.
  • Time, confidence, and independent relevance ratings against a usual-shopping baseline.
  • A live per-agent trace screenshot; the app currently exposes aggregate usage only.

With another month

  • Run a moderated pilot and improve the weakest journey steps.
  • Track search freshness, errors, and cost by role with privacy limits.
  • Add an aggregate spend cap and production-grade account/privacy controls before public release.

Takeaway: Tee:dentity demonstrates a working path from stated taste to a shirt brief. Whether people find that path valuable remains the next question to test.
